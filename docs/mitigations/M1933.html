M1933

Keep a supplier-specific incident playbook with standing risk and access detail

Generic incident response plans do not carry the facts you need in the first
hour of a supplier compromise. Maintain a per-supplier playbook that a
responder can open before contacting anyone, holding:

1. The supplier's risk profile as assessed by risk management with the
business owner: safety or environmental impact, what network/system/data
access they hold, criticality of the service or component, data categories
hosted (IP, personal data, operational configuration), and depth in your
supply chain.
2. The business stakeholders who must be told, and the supplier's security
and commercial points of contact.
3. The standing answer to "what connections do they have into us" — VPN with
or without MFA and proxy, ingress/egress data feeds, IT and privileged
accounts, and vendor paths into OT environments including how updates
reach them.
4. The question set to work through with the supplier, and the pre-agreed
escalation path when answers are generic or slow.

Review the access section whenever a contract changes or a supplier is
onboarded or offboarded: the connections drift, and an incident is the worst
time to discover that. Rehearse the playbook in a joint desktop exercise with
the supplier, sized to their criticality and access.

Source: NCSC ICS Community of Interest Supply Chain Expert Group, "Developing
Incident Response and Management with your Supply Chain" (Colin Topping,
July 2023).

Applies to

  • T0112 Compromised token
  • T0135 Vulnerable CICD system
  • T0209 Compromise package registry or CDN origin
