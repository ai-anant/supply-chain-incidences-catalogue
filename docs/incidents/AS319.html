Poisoned Pipeline / Incidents / AS319

AS319 · 2015-09

Symantec test certificates issued for domains without owner knowledge

Certificates / trust stores

On 17 September 2015 Google announced that a Symantec-issued certificate
for google.com without Google's knowledge had been found. Symantec's own
incident report, published 13 October 2015, disclosed 23 test certificates
covering five organizations including Google and Opera. Using Certificate
Transparency logs, Google found more questionable certificates within
minutes; Symantec's follow-up audit on 12 October 2015 reported 164
certificates over 76 domains and 2,458 certificates for domains that were
never registered. Google required Certificate Transparency for all
Symantec-issued certificates from 1 June 2016, and after a further 2017
failure (distinction misissuance found by Ryan Sleevi) imposed
progressive distrust culminating in Symantec's TLS business being sold
to DigiCert in late 2017.

Mapped techniques

  • T0218 Compromise TLS trust identity

supply-chain Symantec issuance

T0218 · Defense Evasion · Compromise TLS trust identity

Certificates for domains issued without the owner's knowledge are
valid to every client. No breach of CA keys is needed — the CA's
own issuance process is the compromised stage.

Framework gaps noted

  • The 2017 separate Symantec misissuance wave is the same operation's continuation and is folded into this story rather than a new ID.
